## Harmony
Funk is harmonically the simplest of the 13 — the "one-chord vamp" is the defining move: lock onto a single dominant-7 or dominant-13 chord (e.g. G7, G13) and let rhythm carry all the interest, per James Brown's "The One" philosophy ("Sex Machine," "Cold Sweat"). When there is movement, it's typically I7-IV7 vamps or brief ii-V motion, not full jazz changes. Dominant Mixolydian is the default improvisational/harmonic language (major scale with a flatted 7th) — matches the code's 'mixo' scale choice well. Borrowed chords are rare; the "borrowing" in funk is textural (clavinet timbre, wah) rather than harmonic.

## Melody
Melody in funk is largely rhythmic — short, repeated riffs/stabs (horn hits, clavinet comps) rather than long developed lines. When a lead line appears it's tightly syncopated and riff-based, frequently call-and-response between instruments (bass answers horn stab, guitar scratch answers clavinet). Phrases are short (1-2 bars) and looped rather than developed over 4-8 bars like jazzier subgenres.

## Rhythm/groove
Syncopation is the genre's core mechanism: emphasis on off-beats and "the one," 16th-note-dense bass and guitar parts filling the space after beat 1, and ghost notes (soft, buried snare/bass hits) that add inner motion without adding accented weight. A funk drum part typically combines muted guitar scratches, syncopated bass figures, and ghosted snare hits — "the groove IS the progression." This is a fundamentally different swing mechanism than jazz 8th-note swing; it's about note density and dynamic contrast (ghost vs. accent) more than long-short timing ratios.

## Arrangement/structure
Vamp-based, riff-repetition structure — intro riff establishes the groove, verse/chorus distinctions are minimal (often just instrumentation additions: horns enter, clavinet drops out), and the track rides the pocket with textural layering changes rather than harmonic ones. Typical layering: drums + bass groove first, then rhythm guitar/clavinet stabs, then horns/lead on top, with call-and-response breaks.
