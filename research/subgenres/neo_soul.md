## Harmony
Neo-soul is defined by extended, "floating" voicings — m9, maj13, and sus-add9 chords voiced wide across two octaves rather than in tight jazz-club clusters. Quartal voicings (built in stacked 4ths rather than 3rds) add the genre's characteristic unresolved, hovering quality, distinct from the tertian (3rd-stacked) voicings used elsewhere in this set. Many neo-soul tracks (D'Angelo's "Untitled," Erykah Badu's work) sit on just one or two chords for extended stretches, with a chromatically-walking bass line underneath providing the harmonic motion rather than the chords themselves. The V7b9 is a signature pivot chord for resolving into minor tonics with emotional weight, and iii-vi-ii-V is a common neo-soul-specific progression not typically found in the pure-jazz subgenres here.

## Melody
Melody plays second fiddle to groove and voicing color — vocal-style phrasing (even on instrumental leads) with blue-note bends, grace-note slides into chord tones, and a laid-back rhythmic placement that sits noticeably behind the beat, mirroring the vocal delivery style of D'Angelo/Badu-era neo-soul singers.

## Rhythm/groove
The "Dilla Time" concept is neo-soul's rhythmic backbone as much as hip_hop_lofi's: intentional, per-element micro-timing shifts (kicks/snares/hats nudged by milliseconds off-grid) blending straight-quantized and swung-jazz feels into a "simple-complex," humanized groove — famously described as sounding like "a drunk three-year-old hitting the kick" (Questlove on Dilla). This is a distinct rhythmic mechanism from lo_fi_funk's ghost-note syncopation or lofi_jazz's swung-8ths feel — it's asymmetric micro-timing applied per drum voice, not a uniform swing percentage.

## Arrangement/structure
Live-band instrumentation logic even in programmed form: Rhodes/Wurlitzer keys, bass, and drums establish the groove first; jazzy guitar or muted-trumpet motifs punctuate rather than lead continuously; gospel-harmony backing textures and subtle modulation/tape saturation round out the mix. Song structure favors groove-sustained vamps over verse/chorus contrast, consistent with the genre's roots in live jam-informed R&B.
